About Me

My name is Austin Reddy. I am a philosopher and theologian from Tacoma, Washington. I received my B.A. in Philosophy, Politics, and Economics from the University of Washington Tacoma and my M.A. in Religion from Yale Divinity School, where I concentrated in philosophy of religion and philosophical theology.

My research focuses primarily on the aporias of postcolonial politics and the political-theological constitution of law and economics. I am particularly interested in the politics of jouissance, the problem of ideology, and the contested space we call “the political.” My work draws on thinkers such as Lacan, Foucault, Deleuze, and Guattari to reconsider received ideas in political theory—including Marxist, postliberal, and post-Hobbesian traditions.

One of my central interests is the relationship among race, biopolitics, language, and the formation of desire. Although language operates as a quasi-universal structure, it is taken up differently according to each subject’s singular cultural conditions and the discourses surrounding them. I am interested in how these differences situate desire and complicate the subject’s position in both politics and the psychoanalytic clinic.

My work also examines how psychoanalysis confronts racism and whether its emancipatory possibilities can extend beyond the Judeo-Christian and Western traditions in which it developed. My own Indo-Fijian American heritage informs these questions and my broader engagement with postcolonial thought.

I am currently studying for the LSAT and hope to become both a lawyer and a professor of law. Through these pursuits, I aim to contribute to the continuing struggle for justice, equity, and fairness.
